1. About the APRS Protocol

1.1 APRS (Automatic Packet Reporting System) is a real-time tactical digital communication protocol developed by Bob Bruninga (WB4APR) for exchanging information among a large number of stations over a wide area. APRS was originally based on the AX.25 packet radio protocol and operates on VHF frequencies (144.390 MHz in North America, 144.800 MHz in Europe) at 1200 bps using AFSK (Audio Frequency Shift Keying).

1.2 The APRS protocol specifications cover various data formats, including position reports, weather data, messages, telemetry, status, objects, items, queries, and bulletins. APRS v1.0 was released in June 2000, v1.1 was updated in July 2004, and the protocol continues to evolve.

1.3 APRS differs from conventional packet radio in four key aspects: ① integration of maps and other data displays; ② use of a one-to-many broadcast protocol for real-time updates; ③ use of generic digital relays without requiring prior knowledge of network topology; ④ since 1997, interconnection of global users through a transparent Internet backbone.

1.4 As an APRS client, this software sends and receives data in accordance with the above protocol specifications. All data you send through this software follows the APRS protocol format and may be received, stored, and forwarded by other nodes on the global APRS network.

2. About the APRS-IS Internet Service

2.1 APRS-IS (APRS-Internet Service) is an Internet backbone network maintained by volunteer amateur radio operators worldwide, connecting APRS radio networks around the globe.

2.2 This software is a pure client tool and does not operate any APRS servers. All APRS data you send and receive through this software is transmitted via third-party APRS-IS public networks. We assume no responsibility for the availability, accuracy, or security of those networks.

2.3 The APRS-IS network is operated and maintained by volunteers. All users shall comply with the following basic rules:

· Every APRS-IS packet may be forwarded to radio networks, so beacon rates and packet content must comply with RF usage norms;
· Beacon rates should follow reasonable RF frequency standards (mobile stations no less than 1 minute, weather stations no less than 5 minutes, fixed stations no less than 10 minutes, local infrastructure no less than 20 minutes);
· It is prohibited to inject non-amateur-radio content into APRS-IS while masquerading as an amateur station;
· Access to APRS-IS is limited to operators holding a valid amateur radio callsign.

2.4 We do not provide, operate, or recommend any APRS server address, and we do not provide any Internet service such as APRS-IS, iGate, data upload, data relay, or data storage. The software ships with no server address or port: on first launch and under default settings it connects to no APRS-IS, iGate, or other network server. Whether to connect, and to where, is entirely yours to enter and decide.Before the first server connection the software shows a Data & Network Notice, and connects only after you confirm it.

2.5 We have no affiliation, agency, or cooperation with any third-party server. If you configure and connect to one yourself, you must confirm that the server and your use of it comply with the law, and you bear all responsibility arising from cross-border data transfer, personal-data processing, radio regulation, and the third party's terms of service.

4. Data Storage and Privacy

4.1 APRS is by nature a public position-reporting system. APRS data is transmitted in plaintext over amateur radio frequencies and the Internet, and can be received and decoded by anyone. APRS is never encrypted and never private.

4.2 Location tracks, weather data, and messages collected by the APRS-IS network are public data. All APRS data you send through this software may be received, stored, forwarded, and publicly archived by other nodes on the APRS-IS network as well as third-party services (such as aprs.fi).

4.3 If you do not wish your location to be known to anyone in the world, please do not send it via APRS or the Internet.

4.4 Local Data Storage: To ensure normal operation, this software stores the following data locally on your device:

· Beacon history and track points;
· Sent and received messages;
· Address book (callsigns and notes);
· Custom notes and theme settings for stations;
· Software configuration parameters (e.g., server address, beacon interval, map layer preferences, etc.).

4.5 All the above data is stored only locally on your device and is not automatically uploaded to any server controlled by us or any third party. You can delete this local data by using the "Clear Data" function within the software or by uninstalling the software.

4.6 We do not actively collect, store, or process your personal data. The APRS data you send through this software is transmitted directly via third-party APRS-IS networks and does not pass through any server under our control.

4.7 You are responsible for safeguarding your device against unauthorised access to your local data. The development team assumes no liability for data disclosure resulting from loss, theft, or unauthorised use of your device.

4.8 Do not send state secrets, trade secrets, personal data, or sensitive location information over APRS. APRS data may travel in the clear over radio and public networks and may be received, stored, or publicly archived by third parties.
